## Who
Dutch municipalities and their organic waste (GFT) processors, and the households sorting into the green
bin. Roughly a third of Dutch residents put GFT in the wrong bag; the biggest contaminants are plastic
bags, cat litter and "compostable" packaging that does not actually compost in the processor's cycle.

## What they do today instead
Households read the packaging claim ("biobased", "compostable", a seedling logo) and guess. Municipalities
run awareness campaigns and publish do/don't lists on their websites and the Milieu Centraal site.
Processors sample incoming loads; when contamination exceeds their threshold (around 2% is needed for
saleable compost; recent averages are near 3.9%) the load is rejected and burned as residual waste,
and the municipality pays. Some municipalities have moved to inspecting bins by hand and refusing to
empty contaminated ones.

## Why it's unsolved
The signal at the moment of decision (standing at the bin with a coffee pod or a bioplastic bag) is
absent; the guidance lives on a website nobody opens then. Packaging claims are not standardised and
often true only for industrial composting cycles longer than Dutch processors run. Processors and
municipalities do not share contamination data back to the neighbourhoods that caused it. Every
municipality repeats the same campaign independently.
