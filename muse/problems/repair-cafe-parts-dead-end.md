## Who
Volunteer fixers at community repair events (Repair Café, Restart Parties) and the people who bring them
small electricals: vacuum cleaners, kettles, coffee machines, lamps, laptops. Data from the Open Repair
Alliance covers more than 100,000 logged repair attempts across these groups.

## What they do today instead
With the device open on the bench, the volunteer searches supplier sites and forums on their phone for a
part number, a schematic or a teardown, typically within a 20 to 30 minute slot. If nothing turns up they
write "spare part not available" or "needs more time" on the paper form and the owner takes the device
home, where it usually ends up in the bin. Around half of items get fixed; about a quarter are declared
end of life, and 18% are logged as repairable but not repaired on the day for lack of a part, time or skill.
The main reasons for failure are, in order: parts too expensive, parts unavailable, no way to open the
product, no repair information.

## Why it's unsolved
Parts and manuals are scattered across manufacturer portals, third-party sellers and hobbyist wikis, with
no shared index by device model. Repair data is collected on paper or into separate national systems, and
the Open Repair Alliance dataset records outcomes but not "where the part could have been found". Fixers
are volunteers with no time to curate. Manufacturers have little incentive to publish. The EU right-to-repair
rules are arriving but do not create the lookup.
